CO₂-Free Electrochemical Ironmaking Below 600 °C (ongoing)

Problem + Goal

Conventional ironmaking is CO₂-intensive; this project develops an electrochemical route that reduces iron ore to metal below 600 °C, producing only oxygen gas at the anode.

Concept

This project explores a molten-salt electrochemical route to reduce iron ore to metallic iron below 600 °C, using a sintered Fe₂O₃ pellet as the cathode and a stable oxygen-evolving anode so that oxygen gas is the only gaseous by-product. The goal is to enable low-carbon “green steel” without direct greenhouse-gas emissions from the reactor.

What I am building

  1. Cell and reactor design

    Design and assemble high‑temperature molten‑salt electrolysis cells for relatively high temperature operation. (550-600 °C)

  2. Electrode and sample preparation

    Press and sinter Fe₂O₃ pellets to serve as cathodes with controlled density and geometry.
    Prepare and mount oxygen‑evolving anodes; design electrode fixtures and current‑collector assemblies.

  3. High‑temperature operation

    Program furnace ramp/soak profiles and run multi‑hour experiments under controlled atmosphere.
    Monitor and adjust temperature, pressure, and gas composition during electrolysis.

  4. Electrochemical measurements

    Perform bulk electrolysis runs to drive Fe₂O₃ reduction.
    Use complementary techniques (e.g., EMF, CV, EIS, CA/CP) to map operating windows, kinetics, and transport behavior.

  5. Post‑test characterization

    Cross‑section and prepare samples after runs.
    Characterize cathode, anode, and electrolyte with SEM/EDS, XRD, and ICP‑based analysis to track phase changes, metallization, and impurity levels.

  6. Analysis and modeling

    Use thermodynamic tools (e.g., FactSage/HSC) to evaluate stability, activities, and feasible reaction paths.
    Analyze current efficiency, overpotentials, and microstructure–process relationships to guide new experiments.

  7. Documentation, safety, and iteration

    Maintain detailed run logs, sample‑tracking records, and lab protocols.
    Troubleshoot furnace, vacuum, and thermocouple issues; refine cell design and procedures based on each experimental cycle.

Early Results

  • Established a reproducible lab‑scale molten‑salt electrolysis platform for iron‑ore reduction.
  • Process design and investigate thermodynamic & thermochemical properties of oxide specious in molten chloride salt using
  • emf measurements and phase stability analysis
  • Demonstrated substantial metallization of Fe₂O₃ pellets under selected conditions, with oxygen gas as the only intended anodic by‑product.
  • Developed a complete workflow—from pellet fabrication and reactor operation to microstructural and compositional analysis—that now serves as the baseline for further optimization and future scale‑up studies.
