Stability of Perovskite Indoor Photovoltaics : A Focused Review and Call for Standards

Problem + Goal

Perovskite indoor photovoltaics are reaching record efficiencies but lack consistent stability data, so this review maps degradation pathways and proposes a framework for standardized testing and reporting.

Concept

The paper surveys the state of perovskite indoor PVs, analyzes how materials, interfaces, and device architectures influence stability under realistic indoor conditions, and highlights how rarely studies use formal protocols such as ISOS. It then calls for community‑wide stability standards tailored to indoor environments to bridge the gap between lab demonstrations and commercial deployment.

Key Takeaways

  • IPVs now rival or surpass other thin‑film technologies for indoor efficiency (>40%), but fewer than half of papers report any stability testing and only a small fraction follow ISOS‑type protocols.​
  • Stability is strongly controlled by materials choice (bandgap and composition), interface passivation, device architecture, and encapsulation strategy, and these must be co‑designed for long‑lived IPVs.​
  • The review proposes using adapted ISOS dark and light tests plus accelerated, indoor‑relevant stress conditions as a starting point for standardized lifespan assessment.
