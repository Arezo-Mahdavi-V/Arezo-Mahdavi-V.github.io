Anti-Solvent Engineering for Ambient Perovskite Solar Cells

Problem + Goal

Conventional perovskite solar cells need controlled environments to avoid defective films, so this project aims to use anti‑solvent engineering to make efficient, stable devices entirely in ambient air.

Concept

This project shows how careful anti‑solvent engineering can produce high‑quality halide perovskite thin films and ~20% efficient solar cells fabricated entirely under ambient conditions. We compare three anti‑solvents—ethanol (a green solvent), chlorobenzene, and dichlorobenzene—and study how they change film microstructure, defect density, and device performance.

What I built

  1. Thin‑film fabrication in ambient: Prepared double‑cation perovskite precursor solutions and spin‑coated planar heterojunction devices, applying EtOH, CB, or DCB during the spin‑coating step and optimizing the annealing profile.

  2. Microstructure and phase analysis: Used FE‑SEM and XRD to link grain size, pinholes, film roughness, and crystallinity to the chosen anti‑solvent.

  3. Optoelectronic characterization: Helped collect and interpret UV‑Vis, steady‑state PL, and TRPL data to connect carrier lifetime and non‑radiative recombination with film quality.

  4. Device physics and performance: Contributed to JV measurements plus transient techniques (TPC/TPV, dark JV, capacitance–voltage, and EIS) to understand charge transport, recombination, and built‑in potential.

  5. Energy‑level and interface analysis: Assisted with interpreting XPS/UPS results to map band alignment and explain trends in open‑circuit voltage for each anti‑solvent system.

Key Findings

  • Dichlorobenzene produces densely packed, pinhole‑free perovskite films with improved crystallinity compared with chlorobenzene and ethanol.
  • DCB‑treated devices achieve around 20% power‑conversion efficiency with higher Voc and fill factor, thanks to longer carrier lifetimes, suppressed non‑radiative recombination, and lower leakage currents.
  • An optimized choice of anti‑solvent enables a fully ambient, solution‑processed route that is a cost‑effective path toward stable, high‑performance perovskite solar cells.
