Enhancing β‑Phase PVDF Nanofibers for Self‑Powered Sensing

Problem + Goal

Conventional PVDF nanofibers need complex treatments to reach high β‑phase content and strong piezo response, so this project aims to use an ionic liquid surfactant to boost β‑phase and energy output via simple solution mixing.

Concept

The study designs flexible PVDF/ionic‑liquid‑surfactant composite nanofibers as thin, electroactive layers for self‑powered sensing and energy harvesting. By adding a self‑synthesized ionic liquid surfactant (ILS) to PVDF solutions and electrospinning them, the local ionic fields promote β‑phase formation without any extra poling or stretching.

Key Findings

  • Incorporating 4 wt% ILS yielded ≈98.6% β‑phase in PVDF nanofibers and significantly altered fiber morphology.
  • The best composite increased output voltage by ~187% and power density by ~275% compared with neat PVDF, despite thicker fibers.
  • Ionic‑liquid modification provides a straightforward route to highly sensitive, flexible piezoelectric layers for wearable sensors and self‑powered devices.
